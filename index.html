Streamflow Prediction at Ungauged Basins in Mainland Portugal

A data-driven regionalization framework combining catchment descriptors with machine learning for robust streamflow estimation across ungauged sites.

Abstract

Accurate streamflow estimation at ungauged basins is one of the most pressing challenges in operational hydrology. For mainland Portugal — a region with marked hydroclimatic gradients, high inter-annual variability and a significant fraction of ephemeral streams — the reliable quantification of streamflow at sites with no direct observations has important implications for water resources planning, flood risk assessment and ecological management.

This study presents a comprehensive regionalization framework that links hydrological response signatures (mean annual runoff, flow duration curve indices, low-flow and high-flow quantiles) to physiographic, climatic and land-use catchment descriptors through several machine learning algorithms. We evaluate model performance using a rigorous leave-one-out cross-validation scheme across a network of approximately 650 gauged catchments, and we quantify the relative importance of individual predictors. Spatial maps of predicted streamflow statistics for ungauged locations are produced for the full mainland Portuguese territory.

Results show that gradient-boosted trees and random forests achieve the highest accuracy across most target variables, substantially outperforming classical multiple-regression approaches. Climatic descriptors — particularly mean annual precipitation and potential evapotranspiration — are the dominant predictors, while soil and geology features gain importance for low-flow estimation. The supplementary material provided on this page includes all figures, tables, and datasets referenced in the paper.

Data Collection & Preprocessing

Daily streamflow records from the Portuguese National Water Resources Information System (SNIRH) were quality-controlled and gap-filled. Catchment boundaries were delineated using a 25 m digital elevation model. More than 30 physiographic, climatic and land-use descriptors were extracted for each gauged catchment.

2

Signature Computation

Twelve streamflow signatures were computed for each gauged station, including mean annual runoff, flow duration curve percentiles (Q5, Q50, Q95), baseflow index, flashiness index and seasonal runoff ratios. Signatures summarize the characteristic behaviour of each catchment.

3

Machine Learning Regionalization

Five algorithms — multiple linear regression, regression trees, random forests, gradient-boosted trees and support vector regression — were trained to predict each signature from catchment descriptors. Hyperparameter tuning was performed via nested cross-validation.

4

Evaluation & Uncertainty

Model performance was evaluated using leave-one-out cross-validation. Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency, relative bias and RMSE were computed for each signature and algorithm. Prediction intervals were estimated using quantile regression forests.

5

Spatial Mapping

The best-performing model for each signature was applied to predict streamflow statistics at all ungauged locations across mainland Portugal using a 1 km² gridded catchment-descriptor dataset, producing continuous spatial maps of streamflow regimes.
